Executive Summary

Recommendation: pursue NexaLearn as a Tier 1 opportunity with a "Stripe + Yuno" strategy.

  • Situation. NexaLearn ($89M TPV, ~2.1M transactions) will decide in six weeks whether to renew Stripe (1 August), replace it, or add a layer on top. 81% of its volume sits in markets approving only 73–81% of payments, mainly because of cross-border processing, and its homegrown router has no failover (~$180K lost in one outage) DC.
  • Strategy. We do not fight the renewal. NexaLearn renews Stripe on short, non-exclusive terms and keeps its discount; Yuno orchestrates on top, starting with a free 60-day pilot in India and Nigeria. New markets launch on Yuno from day one, and failover is live from go-live.
  • Business case C. On fees alone, Stripe + Yuno lands close to a Stripe-only renewal (between $41K cheaper and $149K more expensive) and $154–344K below today. The value is recovered revenue: $1.05M (risk-adjusted) to $3.72M (base) per year, a net benefit vs. renewing alone of $0.87–3.65M per year, with payback in ~2 months (Yuno-led integration) or ~5.5 months in the worst case. Even risk-adjusted, it exceeds the CFO’s 20% target (34% of current payments cost).
  • Pricing. $0.08 per transaction + 0.30% — the bottom of Yuno’s range, with no discount below it — plus a 0.10% success fee per region, paid only once the uplift is proven. Risk-sharing, not a guarantee.
  • Deal structure. Option A: free pilot, with a 24-month commitment if jointly defined success criteria are met. Option B: pilot at 50% of the fee, with a 12-month commitment. Pilot agreement signed before 1 August; go/no-go at day 90–100.
  • Path to yes. Priya (champion) → Rajiv (technical sponsor) → Amara (business case presented by both) → Tan (signature), with an early call between Yuno’s CEO or GM and Tan.
  • Critical unknowns. Real volume and transaction count (dossier data is inconsistent), the written terms of Stripe’s offer, actual PSP invoices, and onboarding with local acquirers.
  • What I need from you. Approval of the free pilot and commitment terms (deal desk); a Solutions Engineer from week 1; an executive sponsor for the call with Tan; the Partnerships team for local acquirer onboarding.

1. Deal Qualification & Strategic Assessment

Verdict: qualified, Tier 1. NexaLearn combines quantified pain, a compelling event (PSP renewal on 1 August and Q3/Q4 expansion) D and a stack that Yuno improves without replacing. We will not fight Stripe for the renewal. We change the question from "Stripe or Yuno?" to "Stripe plus Yuno": NexaLearn renews Stripe on short, non-exclusive terms; Yuno becomes the orchestration layer, starting with a free 60-day pilot in India and Nigeria and then extending to the other loss-making markets; new markets launch on Yuno from day one; automatic failover is live from go-live. If the pilot meets jointly agreed criteria, a pre-agreed volume commitment activates (Section 5).

Why this is a good opportunity

  • The problem is cross-border processing. 81% of TPV ($72M of $89M) sits in Southeast Asia, India, Africa and LatAm, with approval rates of 73–81% vs. 86–88% in Europe and North America DC. Issuers decline foreign-acquired transactions more often; these soft declines are what local acquiring and local payment methods recover. Examples: India 14% soft declines and no UPI; Brazilian cards acquired via Stripe’s US entity and no Pix; Nigerian cards declined at 29% D.
  • Revenue at stake: 1.5–5 points of approval uplift in those four regions would recover roughly $2.1M (conservative) to $3.7M (base) of gross revenue per year C — illustrative, refined in Section 3.
  • Fragile routing: the homegrown router has no failover, retries or A/B testing; the March Stripe outage cost ~$180K in four hours D.
  • Expansion depends on local payment methods: 8 new markets with no extra engineering headcount D. Without orchestration, each market means another PSP integration.
  • Growth pressure: Finance is pacing toward $103M (+16%) while the board expects 80%+ growth DC. Expansion speed is a board-level issue, not just an operational one.
  • Fit with Yuno: the deal maps to all three value pillars — recover revenue, reduce cost, remove engineering dependency. At Yuno’s standard pricing range ($0.08–0.15/tx + 0.3–0.6%) D, ~2.1M transactions and $89–112M TPV represent a mid-six-figure annual contract that grows with expansion C.

4. Objection Handling

Principle: we never promise what we cannot prove. Every answer relies on NexaLearn’s own data, public evidence or a contractual mechanism — and on what Yuno customers say publicly, not on guarantees. Customer results below are as published in Yuno’s case studies (vendor-reported, mostly LatAm); we present them as their experience, not as a forecast for NexaLearn.

A — Rajiv (CTO): "You’re a 4-year-old startup… what if Yuno goes down or out of business?"

Acknowledge. The right question from the person who lived the March outage and is paged when payments fail.

Substance. Yuno does not replace Stripe: Stripe stays connected underneath and NexaLearn keeps its own acquirer contracts H. Yuno is a PCI DSS Level 1 provider with a documented process to export card tokens to another PCI-certified provider — the exit path is written down before signing P10 11. Backed by a16z, Tiger Global and DST P5, with an office in Singapore P4. Uptime commitments and service credits go into the contract H.

Customer voice. Rappi runs 20+ payment providers through Yuno; adding a provider no longer takes months of development, and payment-issue detection went from 5–10 minutes to milliseconds P12.

Reframe. The concentration risk today is not Yuno; it is one provider. Stripe handles 78% of volume and owns Paystack P1, so ~88% of payments depend on one group C. The March outage cost ~$180K in four hours because nothing failed over D. Orchestration reduces vendor dependency; it does not add to it.

Tie-back. Rajiv stops building and maintaining payments infrastructure — the goal he set himself.

B — Amara (CFO): "Stripe offers 10% off with zero switching cost… what’s the payback, and what if the uplift doesn’t happen?"

Acknowledge. Stripe’s offer is a clean, low-risk number, and protecting the board’s profitability target is your job.

Substance. You don’t have to choose: renew Stripe on short, non-exclusive terms and keep the discount; Yuno sits on top and takes only the volume Stripe struggles with. Risk-adjusted (half our conservative case), the net benefit vs. renewing alone is ~$873K a year; payback ~2 months with a Yuno-led integration, ~5.5 months even at your $400K estimate C. Existing subscriptions stay on Stripe — no big-bang migration.

De-risk. If the uplift is zero, our success fee is not paid and the extra cost over renewing alone is capped at ~$77K a year C. The pilot has exit criteria agreed with you before day one.

Customer voice. Open English, an online education company like NexaLearn: "By enhancing our payment processing, we have increased our approval rates and reduced costs." — Wilmer Sarmiento, Co-Founder P13.

Tie-back. Every number is in a workbook you can rebuild with your own assumptions.

C — Priya (Head of Payments): "They all promise smart routing and +0.5%… Can you guarantee 3 points?"

Acknowledge. You’ve been through that, and you are the one who must defend the result internally.

Substance. We won’t guarantee 3 points — nobody can honestly do so before seeing your decline codes. What makes this case different from a generic smart-routing pitch is that NexaLearn’s losses come from a cause orchestration addresses directly: cross-border processing. Indian cards show 14% soft declines, Brazilian cards are acquired through a US entity, Nigerian cards are declined at 29% D. Moving to local acquiring typically adds 5–15 points D; our model assumes only 1.5–5 C.

Customer voice. inDrive splits volume between providers and compares cost and approval rates, reaching "an approval rate of around 90%" — Vasiliy Everstov, Head of FinTech P14. Livelo reports +5% approval and 50% of failed transactions recovered P15 — relevant while NexaLearn still recovers failed renewals by email.

Prove it, don’t promise it. A 60-day pilot on part of your India and Nigeria traffic, measured by decline code against a Stripe control group — narrow scope, not a 6-month integration. The pilot is free of charge (Section 5), and our success fee applies only once the agreed uplift is proven.

Tie-back. Board-ready evidence for your business case, without hiring engineers.

D — Rajiv (CTO): "My team is slammed with the Q3 launch… can this wait until Q4 or Q1?"

Acknowledge. The Q3 launch comes first, and slow hiring makes every sprint precious.

Substance. The integration is led by Yuno, with our Solutions Engineer doing the heavy lifting. Phase 1 is one integration for new and one-off payments in India and Nigeria; existing subscriptions are untouched. Effort to be estimated by our Solutions Engineer H and planned around your launch freeze.

Customer voice. inDrive launched in 10 new countries in under eight months through a single API P14.

Reframe. Waiting is not free: each quarter of delay leaves ~$220K of risk-adjusted net benefit on the table C; 1 August means signing Stripe on its terms for 12 months; and the 8 new markets in Q4 would each need a new PSP integration — exactly the sprints you want to stop spending.

Tie-back. Starting now is the option that costs your team the fewest sprints.

5.5 Competitive strategy against Stripe

  1. Don’t fight the renewal; absorb it. "Stripe + Yuno" makes Stripe’s discount part of our case.
  2. Answer "zero switching cost" with "zero cost to prove it". The free pilot removes the financial risk of trying Yuno.
  3. Win the timing. Pilot agreement signed before 1 August, so the Stripe renewal can be short and non-exclusive. Priya asks Stripe for its written proposal (term, volume minimums, exclusivity).
  4. Compete on data, not claims. The pilot runs against a Stripe control group where cross-border processing hurts most. We test Stripe’s Indonesia claim instead of disputing it.
  5. A price war helps us. Stripe sits underneath Yuno, so any further Stripe discount also lowers our scenario’s cost C.
  6. Never disparage Stripe. It remains a valued acquirer in the stack; the conversation is about dependency and resilience (~88% of volume with one group) C.
