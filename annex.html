Annex — How This Strategy Was Built

Candidate submission — Yuno Sales Challenge. This annex explains the reasoning behind the proposal and how AI was used to support it.

1. Method

  • Read and reconcile first. Every figure in the dossier was cross-checked before use. Six inconsistencies were found and turned into questions for NexaLearn (Section 1), not ignored.
  • Verify outside the dossier. Public sources were checked live: provider price lists (Stripe, Paystack, Xendit, Razorpay), Visa’s current dispute-monitoring rules, Yuno’s documentation and published case studies.
  • Tag every claim. D dossier, C calculation, P public source, H hypothesis. No assumption is presented as a fact.
  • AI as a tool; decisions stay human. AI was used to extract data, check arithmetic, research and verify sources, build the Excel model and draft text. The strategic choices below were made and challenged by me at each step.

3. Key decisions and why

  1. From "Stripe or Yuno" to "Stripe + Yuno". Competing on Stripe’s price is a losing game. Making Stripe’s discount part of our case neutralises its main argument, and any further Stripe discount also lowers our scenario’s cost.
  2. Selling order: Priya → Rajiv → Amara → Tan. Financial negotiation is where deals usually stall. We reach the CFO with two internal sponsors — Payments and the CTO/co-founder — already behind the pilot.
  3. Honest economics. On fees alone, Stripe + Yuno roughly matches a Stripe-only renewal. We say so upfront and build the case on recovered revenue, using a risk-adjusted scenario a CFO can trust.
  4. No guarantees. The dossier gives no basis to promise an approval-rate uplift, so we don’t. A success fee shares the risk instead.
  5. Price at the bottom of the range, not below it. Competitive without giving the technology away; a volume tier is held back for the close.
  6. The pilot evolved over three iterations. First draft: sign a contract, then pilot — rejected because results would arrive after the decision. Second: run a full 60-day pilot first, with the commitment pre-agreed if it succeeds. Final: a free pilot (Option A), signalling a long-term partnership. Option B was redesigned after calculating that a 50% pilot discount is worth only ~$5K to the client: the real difference between the options had to be the commitment, not the price.
  7. Let customers speak. Public case studies were matched to each stakeholder (Open English, an edtech company, for the CFO). Reference calls are offered only with those customers’ consent.

4. What the data checks revealed

  • Volume: the baseline conflicts across sources ($89M / $95M / $103M), and the transaction count cannot reach 2.1M with the stated price ranges (maximum ~1.29M).
  • Stripe’s offer: its effective rate is ~3.2%, not 2.85%, and its claimed $240K saving does not reconcile with its own pricing.
  • Hidden concentration: Stripe owns Paystack, so ~88% of volume depends on one group.
  • Outdated rule: Visa’s dispute-monitoring rules have changed since the threshold cited in the dossier.
  • Counter-intuitive cost: Indonesian wallets approve better but cost more than cards, so routing to them raises revenue, not savings.
